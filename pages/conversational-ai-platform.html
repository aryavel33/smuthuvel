03

Consequential decisions

I made the tradeoffs that turned the thesis into a viable product

The platform had to perform in real time, earn enterprise trust, remain usable for partners, and fit Cisco’s reseller economics. Three decisions shaped the path.

  1. 01 · Transport

    Expand beyond gRPC to meet the market

    Starting point

    Engineering selected gRPC for the original Google integration and its server-to-server streaming model.

    Market signal

    AI vendors consistently found WebSockets more compatible with the integration patterns already available in the market.

    My leadership

    I turned that feedback into a product requirement and drove the WebSocket release, opening a more familiar path without replacing the gRPC foundation.

  2. 02 · Latency and trust

    Keep the media path direct without giving up control

    Constraint

    Every additional environment could add latency, while cross-cloud media still required strong identity and isolation.

    My decision

    I limited media to Cisco cloud and the vendor cloud. Cisco issues the token even when the vendor invokes the API, simplifying token management and controlling Cisco-side configuration.

    Trust layer

    We introduced mutual TLS so vendors can authenticate that data streams originate from Cisco.

  3. 03 · Commercial model

    Make consumption pricing predictable for the reseller ecosystem

    Constraint

    Reseller commissions depended on predictable three-to-five-year contract value, creating resistance to an open-ended usage bill.

    My decision

    I introduced a monthly committed usage floor. Customers pay the commitment and are billed for overage when consumption exceeds it.

    Result

    Cisco gained usage-based economics while partners retained predictable monthly and annual value for commission planning.
