CRM connector strategy

One native contact-center experience across enterprise CRM platforms

At Cisco, I defined a strategy to simplify a fragmented connector portfolio and create a clear path from legacy CTI integrations to native, AI-ready experiences.

The product

CRM connectors bring Webex Contact Center into the systems where service teams already work. They combine call control, customer context, routing, records, and AI assistance inside the CRM desktop.

02

Strategy and execution

I defined one destination, then organized the portfolio around adoption

Shipping another connector would not solve the portfolio problem. I focused the work on three decisions that connected the product model, customer transition, and market adoption.

  1. 01

    Choose the destination

    Position the CRM-native generation as the target and align voice, digital, supervisor, and AI workflows around one product model.

    One native experience
  2. 02

    Protect the transition

    Close critical parity gaps, preserve voice reliability, and migrate customers only when the target platform was ready.

    Continuity without dead ends
  3. 03

    Win inside the CRM

    Pair CRM-specific customization and AI workflows with a simpler commercial model, enablement, and one go-to-market story.

    Adoption and deal leverage
03

A consequential tradeoff

I reduced portfolio breadth while preserving customer extensibility

V1 supported packaged connectors for Salesforce, ServiceNow, Zendesk, Freshdesk, and Microsoft Dynamics. Investing evenly across all five would have diluted the teams needed to build the next generation.

InputsAdoption and demand

Compared active users by connector with requests entering the customer pipeline.

DecisionEnd-of-life 2 of 5

Concentrated packaged V1 CTI investment on the connectors with the strongest evidence.

ContinuityKeep the SDK path

Maintained deployable desktop connectors through the Webex Developer Portal for custom third-party desktops.

The tradeoff reduced packaged coverage, but focused development capacity while giving customers and partners a supported way to build beyond the prioritized CRM platforms.

04

My product leadership

I owned the portfolio from investment choices through launch

In the first four months, I reset the roadmap across Salesforce, ServiceNow, Zendesk, Microsoft Dynamics, Freshdesk, Epic, and athenahealth. I made the initial investment and end-of-life decisions, then carried the portfolio through its business case, product launch, and offer launch.

Investment allocation
Made the end-of-life and retention decisions that focused engineering capacity on the highest-value connectors.
V3 platform focus
Streamlined development around Salesforce and ServiceNow, where platform-specific architectures required dedicated investment.
Product differentiation
Introduced real-time translation, Model Context Protocol (MCP) and Agent-to-Agent (A2A) integration patterns, and quick-launch templates.
Developer extensibility
Preserved SDK and deployable connector options so customers could build for third-party CRM and non-CRM desktops.
Commercial ownership
Owned the roadmap, business case, product launch, and offer launch as one coordinated portfolio program.
